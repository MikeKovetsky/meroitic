# Lead 4: Meroitic words in Greek, Latin, Demotic and Coptic, and Egyptian words in Meroitic

**Answer first.** This file is the consolidated equation table: every Meroitic word, title or name found in
this pass with an equivalent in another language, one row each, with the direction of borrowing and the
source. It has 36 rows: 14 titles and common words, 13 place and people names, 9 divine and royal names or
titles (personal names are in lead 1). Most titles went *from* Egyptian *into* Meroitic (`pelmos`, `peseto`,
`perite`, `apote`, `tewisti`, `plsn`), so they fix sound values and meanings but are not native vocabulary.
The native words with a meaning fixed by an outside text are few: `qore` (king), `kdke`/`ktke` (Candace),
`qoreñ` and `kroro` (titles whose sense is unknown), `ato` (water, from *pelmos atolis* = Demotic "general
of the water"), `-lh` (great, from *apote-lh* = Demotic *wpte ꜥꜣ*), `Arome` (Rome), and the place names.

The brief's examples need one correction: `peseto` and `pelmos` are not Meroitic titles rendered in
Demotic; both are Egyptian titles borrowed into Meroitic (*pꜣ sꜣ-nsw* "the king's son", *pꜣ mr-mšꜥ* "the
general"), as Griffith proposed and FHN III p. 1009 accepts. For `qore` no Demotic transcription was found:
Demotic texts call the Meroitic king *Pr-ꜥꜣ* "Pharaoh", and the Demotic *ḳrny* renders the derived title
`qoreñ`.

## Patterns visible in the pairs (hypotheses, not published claims checked here)

1. Meroitic `d` stands where Greek, Latin or Demotic has *r*: `Pedeme` / *Primis*; `(Te)qoridmni` /
   Demotic `Tkrrmn`; perhaps `Bedewe` / *Meroe*. Rilly's grammar (2007) discusses the value of `d`; it was not
   read for this note.
2. Meroitic `b` stands where Greek has *m* in `Bedewe` / Μερόη, if that equation holds.
3. Egyptian loans keep the Egyptian article: `pe-lmos` (*pꜣ mr-mšꜥ*), `pe-seto` (*pꜣ sꜣ-nsw*), `pe-rite`
   (*pꜣ rt*), `te-wisti` (*tꜣ wšte*). Griffith 1911 p. 9 used this to set the vowels of `p` and `t`.

## Sources for more pairs of this kind

1. The Greek itineraries of Bion (FHN II nos 105-108) and Juba (FHN III no. 186a) list dozens of Kushite
   towns in Greek spelling; FHN III Table C matches only the ones above (after Priese 1984, print only).
2. Greek and Demotic personal names of Meroites in the Dodecaschoenus: Burkhardt 1985 identifies 36
   "Meroitic" Demotic graffiti by their non-Egyptian names (Ashby p. 14). Griffith 1911 p. 11 lists the
   letters these names use. A systematic list of those names against Meroitic names in the REM corpus was
   not found and would be worth building.
3. The Old Nubian comparison (Rilly 2010, *Le méroïtique et sa famille linguistique*) is a separate,
   comparative line, not bilingual evidence; not covered here.
